01About

Kolkata, where I grew up, ranks among the large cities least prepared for a changing climate. Each year the heat climbed higher and the monsoon floods reached further, and the people with the least say in how the city was built usually paid the steepest price. Those years are the root of my interest in Human-Climate Interactions: how a warming climate, choices about land and development, and long-standing social inequality combine to decide who stays safe.

I trained first as a historian, focusing on environmental and ecological history, until maps drew me into geography. At the Field Museum in Chicago, my research focused on how stormwater infiltrated areas with native plant species versus turf grass areas. At Indiana State University, I incorporated GIS tools into urban geography theories, mapping flood risk zip code by zip code across Chicago and finding it concentrated in disinvested neighborhoods that are home to many BIPOC (Black, Indigenous, and People of Color) residents.

Since 2023, I have worked as a GIS analyst at SPAARC, shifting from the city to the coast. I build the spatial data and maps that document wetland loss in coastal Louisiana, where a century of levees, canal dredging and oil and gas development has reshaped the delta, including work that supports the state's coastal land-loss litigation. I am now applying to Geography PhD programs to carry these questions into urban and political geography and climate justice.
